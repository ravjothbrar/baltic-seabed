HOW IT WORKS

Watching the seabed
from the surface.

Baltic Seabed Watch is a live map of the ships moving over the Baltic's undersea cables and pipelines. It fuses public ship tracking, sanctions lists and infrastructure routes into a small set of explainable signals. Every flag carries the reasons it is probably innocent, because surface data alone has never settled one of these cases. The last layer shows what listening below the surface would add.

01

Architecture

One engine, written once, runs in the browser (or on a server). Public sources feed it; GitHub Actions keeps the slow-moving data fresh. There is no private data and no API key in the page.

02

Seven signals, each with its excuse

Every rule is plain code with a threshold you can read. Each one ships with the ordinary reasons it fires, and those reasons are shown next to every alert.

LOITER behaviour · weak alone

Under 3 kn for 20+ minutes within 1.5 nm of a cable or pipeline, outside port zones, not moored.

Innocent causes: waiting for a berth, a pilot or orders; weather; fishing and work vessels.

DRAG behaviour · moderate alone

A run of consecutive fixes at 1–7 kn, at most 60% of the ship's own usual speed (8 kn+), still "under way", covering 2 km and 10 minutes along a corridor.

Innocent causes: engine trouble, ice, traffic separation, slowing for a pilot.

CROSS-SLOW behaviour · weak alone

Passing within 0.3 nm of a named asset at under half the ship's usual speed (6 kn+).

Innocent causes: busy lanes and approaches cross cables constantly.

DARK behaviour · moderate alone

No AIS for 45 minutes to 6 hours, last seen moving within 10 nm of infrastructure, while 5+ nearby ships kept reporting.

Innocent causes: receiver gaps, transponder faults, message rate limits.

WATCHLIST identity

IMO or MMSI on OpenSanctions maritime lists (sanctions, shadow-fleet tracking, port-state detentions).

A listing describes ownership or past conduct, not what the ship is doing now.

FOC identity · weak

Flag (from the MMSI) in a registry repeatedly named in shadow-fleet reporting.

Thousands of ordinary ships fly these flags.

PROFILE identity

A tanker or cargo ship tracked leaving a Russian Gulf of Finland port in the last 6 hours, now loitering or dragging. Inbound ships never qualify.

Most such ships are simply trading.

LEVELS how signals combine

WATCH one behaviour signal
ELEVATED behaviour + one more
HIGH three, incl. DRAG or DARK

Explained signals don't count. Identity alone never escalates.

WATCH one unexplained behaviour signal, or a listed vessel inside a corridor.
ELEVATED behaviour plus at least one more unexplained signal.
HIGH three or more, including DRAG or DARK.

Identity signals never escalate an alert on their own. Context checks (gale ≥ 30 kn or waves ≥ 3 m, work and passenger vessels, "waiting for orders", port zones) mark a signal as explained, and explained signals don't count.

03

How it's tested

6 synthetic cases built along real cable geometry (a drag modelled on published Eagle S descriptions, anchoring on a cable, ordinary transit, a trawler, a gale, a port wait) and 6 regression checks for bugs found in review (unavailable speed, a single slow fix, inbound ships, wide-radius gaps, expired alerts, restart replay). All pass on every push.

What's not measured yet: the false-positive rate on real traffic. That needs 48 hours of recorded AIS and 30–40 episodes labelled by hand, not by a model. Until then, treat every threshold as a starting point.

04

What this can't see

  • AIS is self-reported. It can be spoofed, switched off or simply not received.
  • Coverage is uneven. Northern Baltic live (Digitraffic); whole Baltic every 15 min when aisstream is enabled.
  • Satellite radar helps, slowly. Global Fishing Watch detections lag by days and also see small boats, rigs and turbines.
  • Nothing below the surface. No sensor here hears an anchor on the seabed, a diver or a submersible.

That last gap is the point. The glider layer simulates how a few hundred autonomous underwater gliders, tasked by one operator, would cover the corridors that surface data can only guess at. It is a simulation with stated assumptions, not a claim about anyone's system.
